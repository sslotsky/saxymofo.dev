Converting your starter into a sweet stiff levain suppresses sour acid production, giving you a classic, mildly sweet, tender challah with a plush, shreddable crumb and zero dairy.

**Flour option:** For extra softness and flavor, replace the final dough's <Q>400 g</Q> bread flour with <Q>340 g</Q> bread flour and <Q>60 g</Q> white whole wheat flour.

**Eggs:** The whole eggs and yolk weigh about <Q>115 g</Q> total. Save the leftover egg white for the wash!

For a breakfast using this loaf, see [challah French toast](/recipes/challah-french-toast/).

## Stage 1: Sweet Stiff Levain (The Night Before, 10–12 Hours Ahead)

1. Mix <Q>20 g</Q> ripe starter, <Q>60 g</Q> bread flour, <Q>30 g</Q> room-temperature water, and <Q>10 g</Q> sugar or honey into a firm ball. Knead briefly on the counter until smooth, place in a covered jar, and ferment at room temperature (70°F–74°F) overnight until tripled in size and domed.

## Step 1: Mix & Knead (15–20 Minutes)

1. In a large bowl (or stand mixer), whisk <Q>125 g</Q> warm water, <Q>50 g</Q> honey, <Q>2 eggs</Q> plus the yolk from <Q>1 egg</Q>, and <Q>45 g</Q> oil until smooth.
2. Tear all of the ripe sweet levain (about <Q>120 g</Q>) into small pieces and drop them into the liquid to soften for 2–3 minutes.
3. Add <Q>400 g</Q> flour (or the flour option above) and <Q>8 g</Q> salt. Mix until a shaggy dough forms.
4. Knead for 8–10 minutes (by hand on an unfloured counter, or on medium-low speed in a stand mixer) until the dough is smooth, elastic, and passes the windowpane test. Challah dough is firm and slightly tacky at first, then turns silky as the gluten develops.

## Step 2: Bulk Fermentation (5 to 7 Hours at 72°F–75°F)

1. Place the dough in a lightly oiled bowl, cover tightly, and let it ferment at room temperature until it doubles in volume and feels airy and puffy. (Enriched doughs rise slower than lean bread, so judge by volume rather than the clock.)

## Step 3: Divide & Roll Strands (30 Minutes)

1. Turn the dough out onto a clean, unfloured surface (a tiny bit of friction helps roll even ropes).
2. Divide into 4 equal pieces (about <Q>205 g</Q> each) for a classic 4-strand braid, or 3 pieces (about <Q>275 g</Q> each) for a 3-strand braid.
3. Flatten each piece into a small rectangle, roll it up tightly like a cigar, and pinch the seam closed. Cover and rest for 15 minutes so the gluten relaxes.
4. Roll each cylinder from the center outward into a 14- to 16-inch rope, tapering the ends slightly. If the dough snaps back, cover and rest 5 more minutes.

## Step 4: Braid & Final Proof (3 to 4 Hours at Room Temperature, or Overnight Cold Proof)

1. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.
2. Pinch the tops of your strands together and braid loosely—leaving a tiny bit of breathing room so the loaf expands upward rather than splitting at the seams. Pinch and tuck both ends neatly underneath.
3. **First Egg Wash:** Whisk the reserved white from <Q>1 egg</Q> with <Q>1 tsp</Q> water and <Q>1 pinch</Q> salt until frothy. Brush the braided loaf lightly with the reserved egg white mixture (this keeps the surface from drying out while proofing).
4. Cover loosely with an inverted container or greased plastic wrap. Proof at 72°F–75°F for 3 to 4 hours until puffy, jiggly, and visibly expanded by about 75%.
5. **Optional Overnight Schedule:** After braiding, cover tightly and refrigerate overnight (10–14 hours). Take it out the next morning and let it finish proofing at room temperature for 2–3 hours until puffy before baking.

## Step 5: Bake

1. Preheat your oven to 350°F with a rack in the center position.
2. **Second Egg Wash:** Brush the proofed loaf gently a second time with egg wash for a deep, glossy bakery shine, and sprinkle with sesame or poppy seeds if using.
3. Bake for 30 to 38 minutes, rotating the pan halfway through, until the crust is a rich, deep mahogany brown and the internal temperature reaches 190°F–195°F. (If the top browns too quickly around minute 25, tent loosely with foil.)
4. Cool on a wire rack for at least 1 hour before slicing so the tender crumb sets cleanly.
