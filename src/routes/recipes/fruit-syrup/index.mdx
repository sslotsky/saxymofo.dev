Frozen fruit releases juice readily as it cooks. Straining removes the pulp, skins, and seeds so the syrup mixes smoothly into yogurt.

<Variation name="base">

## Velvet strawberry or raspberry syrup

1. Combine the berries, sugar, and water in a small saucepan over medium heat.
2. Bring to a gentle simmer, stirring occasionally until the sugar dissolves and the berries turn pale and mushy, about 8–10 minutes. Do not boil furiously, or the syrup will thicken too much into jam.
3. Remove from the heat and stir in the lemon juice.
4. Pour through a fine-mesh sieve into a glass jar. Do not press or mash the berries with a spoon—let the syrup drip through naturally to keep it clear and seed-free.

</Variation>

<Variation name="Spiced Blueberry-Maple">

## Spiced blueberry-maple syrup

1. Simmer the blueberries, maple syrup, sugar, water, and optional cinnamon in a saucepan over medium heat for 10 minutes, until the berries burst.
2. Remove from the heat, stir in the lemon juice, and steep for 5 minutes.
3. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve into a clean glass bottle or jar. Let gravity do the work so the skins stay behind.

</Variation>

<Variation name="Peach-Vanilla">

## Peach-vanilla syrup

1. Simmer the peaches, sugar, and water over medium heat for 10–12 minutes, until the peaches are completely soft.
2. Take off the heat and stir in the lemon juice and vanilla extract.
3. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve into a jar. Eat or discard the poached peach slices separately so the syrup stays completely liquid.

</Variation>

## Storage and notes

Store the strained syrup in a clean glass mason jar or squeeze bottle in the refrigerator. The source recipe gives a refrigerated shelf life of 3 to 4 weeks, and suggests replacing <Q>2 tbsp</Q> of the white sugar with light corn syrup or agave nectar during cooking to prevent crystallization and extend storage up to 2 months. Those storage times are not a tested preservation guarantee; keep refrigerated and discard if there are signs of spoilage.

Blend leftover strained berry or peach solids into a smoothie with some water or dairy-free milk rather than wasting them.
