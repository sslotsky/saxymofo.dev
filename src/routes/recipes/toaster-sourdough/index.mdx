## Equipment

Two identical 8.5" × 4.5" × 2.75" (1 lb) loaf pans, one large mixing bowl, a damp kitchen towel, a sharp knife or kitchen shears, oven mitts, and a wire cooling rack. The second pan sits upside down over the first, rim-to-rim, to make a steam-trapping dome.

The schedule below is for one loaf. When scaling, divide the dough among additional pans rather than overfilling one pan; keep the pan size and baking temperatures the same.

## Friday night: wake up the starter

1. **9:00 PM:** Take the jar of starter scrapings out of the refrigerator.
2. **9:05 PM:** Feed it a 1:2:2 ratio directly in the jar: stir together <Q>25 g</Q> starter (what's already in the jar), <Q>50 g</Q> lukewarm water, and <Q>50 g</Q> unbleached all-purpose flour. Use all-purpose flour here to save on bread flour.
3. Screw the lid on loosely and leave the jar on the kitchen counter overnight.

## Saturday morning: mix and bulk ferment

1. **8:00 AM — Mix:** Combine <Q>250 g</Q> water and <Q>75 g</Q> bubbly active starter in the large bowl. Whisk with your fingers until milky. Put the remaining jar of starter scrapings straight back into the fridge until next Friday.
2. Add <Q>375 g</Q> bread flour and <Q>8 g</Q> salt. Mix by hand until no dry flour spots remain. Cover with a damp towel and rest for 30 minutes.
3. **8:30 AM–10:30 AM — Stretch and folds:** Every 30 to 45 minutes, grab one edge of the dough, pull it upward, and fold it over the center. Rotate the bowl 90 degrees and repeat three more times. Complete 3 to 4 rounds total over two hours.
4. **10:30 AM–4:00 PM — Bulk rise:** Cover the bowl and leave it on the counter until the dough grows by roughly 50–70%, looks slightly domed, and shows tiny bubbles on the surface. Cool kitchens take longer—trust the growth, not the clock.

## Saturday evening: shape, pan proof, and double-pan bake

1. **4:00 PM — Shape:** Turn the dough onto a lightly floured counter and gently pat it into a flat rectangle about 8 inches wide. Roll it up tightly like a sleeping bag and pinch the final seam shut.
2. **4:15 PM — Pan proof:** Lightly grease the first 8.5" × 4.5" loaf pan. Place the dough inside seam-side down. Cover with a damp cloth and let it rise until the crown swells 1 full inch above the top rim of the pan, usually 2.5 to 4 hours.
3. **7:30 PM — Preheat:** Place an oven rack in the lower-third position and preheat to 420°F (215°C).
4. **8:00 PM — Covered steam bake:** Lightly dust the dough with flour. Make one shallow, quick snip down the center length of the loaf with a sharp knife or kitchen shears. Flip the second empty loaf pan upside down and set it over the filled pan, rim-to-rim. Bake covered for 20 minutes.
5. **8:20 PM — Uncovered crisp:** Carefully remove the inverted pan with oven mitts. Drop the oven temperature to 400°F (205°C) and bake uncovered for 18 to 22 minutes, until the crust is a deep, even mahogany brown.
6. **8:42 PM — Cool down:** Immediately tip the loaf onto a wire cooling rack. Wait at least 2 full hours before slicing so the interior crumb sets properly and doesn't turn gummy.

Once completely cool, slice the whole loaf and store the slices in a freezer bag. Pull them straight out for the toaster all week.
