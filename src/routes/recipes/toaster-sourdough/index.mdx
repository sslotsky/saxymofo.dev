## Equipment & pan setup

- **Twin loaf pans:** Two identical **8.5" x 4.5" x 2.75" (1 lb) aluminized steel loaf pans** (such as USA Pan 1 lb loaf pans). The second pan sits upside down over the first, rim-to-rim, to make a steam-trapping dome.
- **Steam dome lock:** Two **oven-safe steel binder clips** (1.25"–2", plastic/rubber handles removed) or heavy-duty foil strips to lock the inverted top pan rim-to-rim so it cannot slide.
- **Shaping & storage:** A straight-sided starter jar (e.g., Weck 741 1/4 L mold jar) and a flat stainless steel bench scraper (e.g., Dexter-Russell Sani-Safe 6" x 3").

The method is for one loaf. Each loaf needs its own pair of pans and clips for the dome, so when scaling up, use an extra pair per loaf or bake the loaves one after another. Keep the pan size, times and temperatures the same.

## Step 1: Starter prep & clean jar swap

1. **Friday night (night before mixing):** Pull your jar of <Q>25 g</Q> starter scrapings from the fridge. Feed it at a **1:2:2 ratio** with the water and your selected flour blend from the Starter Feed list (<Q>125 g</Q> total). Cover loosely and leave at room temperature overnight until doubled and bubbly.
2. **Clean jar rotation:** Measure <Q>75 g</Q> active starter into your mixing bowl. Spoon ~<Q>25 g</Q> into a fresh, clean jar for next week, discard the rest, and wash the old jar immediately so dried crust never builds up.

<When flour="0-www">

For the classic white loaf, feed your <Q>25 g</Q> starter scrapings with <Q>50 g</Q> lukewarm water and <Q param="feedWhiteFlour" unit="g" /> unbleached all-purpose flour. (If you want a more vigorous fermentation boost, you can swap half or all of the feed flour for white whole wheat.)

</When>

<When flour="25-www">

**50/50 starter feed:** Feed your <Q>25 g</Q> starter scrapings with <Q>50 g</Q> lukewarm water, <Q param="feedWhiteFlour" unit="g" /> unbleached all-purpose flour, and <Q param="feedWwFlour" unit="g" /> white whole wheat flour. The added minerals from the whole grain give the yeast a faster, more reliable overnight rise.

</When>

<When flour="40-www">

**100% white whole wheat starter feed:** Feed your <Q>25 g</Q> starter scrapings with <Q>50 g</Q> lukewarm water and <Q param="feedWwFlour" unit="g" /> white whole wheat flour. Whole wheat acts as rocket fuel for sluggish refrigerated starters—expect it to peak slightly faster overnight.

</When>

## Step 2: Mix & gluten development

1. **Mix:** Whisk <Q>75 g</Q> active starter into <Q param="water" unit="g" /> lukewarm water until milky. Add the flour and <Q>8 g</Q> fine sea salt, mixing by hand until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest 30 minutes.

<When hydration="75">
2. **Coil folds (75% hydration):** Perform **4 rounds of coil folds** spaced 30 minutes apart over 2 hours. Dip hands in water, slide fingers under the center of the dough, lift straight up so the ends hang and coil underneath as you set it back down, and rotate 90° four times per round.
</When>
<When hydration="67">
2. **Stretch & folds (67% hydration):** Perform **3 to 4 rounds of standard stretch-and-folds** spaced 30 minutes apart over 2 hours. Grab one edge, pull upward, and fold over the center; rotate 90° four times per round.
</When>
<When hydration="80">
2. **Gentle coil folds (80% hydration):** Perform **5 rounds of gentle coil folds** spaced 30 minutes apart over 2.5 hours using wet hands. Avoid tearing the thin gluten web as the dough becomes airy and jiggly.
</When>

<When flour="25-www">

Because white whole wheat absorbs more moisture than white flour, this <Q param="water" unit="g" /> water dough will feel noticeably drier, less sticky, and easier to shape than the same hydration with all white flour—roughly one hydration step stiffer. You can use unbleached all-purpose flour for the white portion without losing loaf height.

</When>

<When flour="40-www" hydration="75,80">

White whole wheat bran absorbs significantly more water, so add up to <Q>10 g</Q> more water if the dough feels stiff during mixing. The higher bran content creates a slightly denser, hearty crumb that holds up well to heavy spreads without turning gummy.

</When>

<When flour="40-www" hydration="67">

**Absorption note:** Because 40% white whole wheat bran absorbs significantly more moisture than white flour, <Q param="water" unit="g" /> water (67% hydration) will feel quite stiff. Add an extra <Q>15 g</Q> to <Q>25 g</Q> of water if dry flour pockets remain during mixing. The higher bran content creates a slightly denser, hearty crumb that holds up well to heavy spreads.

</When>

## Step 3: Fermentation, shaping & proofing

<When schedule="same-day">
1. **Room-temperature bulk rise:** Cover the bowl and ferment at room temperature until grown by **50–70%**, domed on top, with an extensive bubble network visible through the sides (typically 4–6 hours after mixing). Cool kitchens take longer—trust the growth, not the clock.
2. **Shape & pan:** Lightly oil one 8.5" x 4.5" loaf pan. Shape the dough into a cylinder (see the shaping guide below) and place seam-side down in the pan.
3. **Final pan proof:** Cover with a damp towel or inverted bowl and let rise at room temperature until the dough crowns **1 full inch above the pan rim** (2.5–4 hours).
</When>
<When schedule="overnight-pan">
1. **Day 1 room-temperature bulk rise:** Ferment at room temperature until grown by **50–70%**, domed on top, with strong side bubbles.
2. **Shape & chill overnight:** Lightly oil one 8.5" x 4.5" loaf pan, shape the dough into a cylinder (see the shaping guide below), and place seam-side down in the pan. Cover tightly with plastic wrap or an inverted bag or bowl so the surface cannot dry out, and refrigerate overnight (8–16 hours).
3. **Day 2 final pan proof:** Pull the covered pan from the fridge and let it sit on the counter until the dough warms up and crowns **1 full inch above the pan rim** (typically **2 to 4 hours**).
</When>
<When schedule="overnight-bulk">
1. **Day 1 head start & chill:** After your final fold, let the dough rest at room temperature for at least **1 hour** so fermentation is underway. Before leaving the house (and before the dough peaks), cover the bowl tightly and place it straight into the fridge overnight. *(If the dough has already grown 50–70% before you leave, shape it into the pan now and switch to the **Overnight Pan Proof** schedule.)*
2. **Day 2 finish bulk & shape:** Pull the cold bowl from the fridge and let it sit at room temperature until bulk rise reaches **50–70% total growth** with strong side bubbles. Shape into a lightly oiled 8.5" x 4.5" loaf pan (see the shaping guide below).
3. **Day 2 final pan proof:** Cover the pan and let rise on the counter until the crown swells **1 full inch above the pan rim** (typically **4 to 6 hours** from cold).
</When>

### Shaping guide

<When hydration="75">
- **Misted counter & envelope roll:** Lightly mist a bare countertop with water and wet your fingertips. Gently turn out the dough, stretch into a loose ~8" rectangle, fold the left and right thirds inward like a business letter, and lightly roll into a cylinder. Slide a **wet bench scraper** underneath to cradle it and drop it seam-side down into the oiled pan.
</When>
<When hydration="67">
- **Flour-the-top flip:** Turn the dough onto a **bare, dry counter** (no flour or water underneath). Lightly dust *only the top* with flour, slide a scraper underneath to flip it floured-side down, pat into an 8" rectangle, and roll tightly into a cylinder so the sticky unfloured side seals to itself. Place seam-side down in the oiled pan.
</When>
<When hydration="80">
- **Light-touch wet transfer:** Mist the counter lightly and keep hands wet. Gently coax the dough into a loose rectangle without degassing, letter-fold the thirds, tuck the ends lightly, and use a wet bench scraper in one confident scooping motion to transfer it seam-side down into the oiled pan.
</When>

## Step 4: Cold-dome steam bake (no pan preheating)

1. **Preheat the oven only:** Position an oven rack in the lower-third slot and preheat to **420°F (215°C)**. Leave the second loaf pan at room temperature on the counter—**do not preheat it**.
2. **Score & clip the dome on the counter:** Dust the crowned dough lightly with flour and make one shallow lengthwise snip down the center with kitchen shears or a sharp knife. Invert the **cool, unheated** second loaf pan rim-to-rim over the filled pan and clamp opposite rims together with **two metal binder clips** (or crimp foil collars) so the dome cannot slip.
3. **Covered steam bake (20 minutes):** Set the clipped pans on a rimmed baking sheet for stability and bake for **20 minutes** at **420°F (215°C)**.
4. **Uncovered bake (18–22 minutes):** Remove the binder clips and lift off the top pan using silicone-gripped oven mitts. Reduce the oven to **400°F (205°C)** and bake uncovered for **18 to 22 minutes** until deep mahogany brown.
5. **Cool completely:** Immediately turn the loaf out onto a wire rack. Wait **at least 2 full hours** before slicing so the crumb sets cleanly for freezing and toasting.

Once completely cool, slice the whole loaf and store the slices in a freezer bag. Pull them straight out for the toaster all week.
