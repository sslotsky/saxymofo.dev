This formula yields one medium-large boule that fills out a 6- to 7.5-quart Dutch oven nicely without spreading flat.

The flour blend is 80% bread flour and 20% white whole wheat. White whole wheat absorbs extra water and boosts wheat flavor while keeping the crumb tender. Water is 76%, starter is 20%, and salt is 2% of the flour weight.

**Same-day option:** Use <Q>2.5 g</Q> instant yeast instead of the sourdough starter for a same-day loaf. The overnight cold-proof schedule below is for the sourdough version.

**Starter tip:** If you're using a store-bought premade starter for a single weekly loaf, feed it 12 hours before mixing so it's at peak dome and bubbly.

For a loaf-pan bake, see [toaster sourdough](/recipes/toaster-sourdough/).

## Step 1: Fermentolyse & Mix (30 Minutes)

1. Whisk <Q>86 g</Q> ripe starter and <Q>326 g</Q> warm water together in a mixing bowl until milky.
2. Add <Q>343 g</Q> bread flour and <Q>86 g</Q> white whole wheat flour and mix until no dry bits remain. Cover and rest for 30 minutes to let the white whole wheat hydrate.
3. Sprinkle <Q>9 g</Q> salt over the dough and pinch/fold it in thoroughly for 2–3 minutes until smooth.

## Step 2: Bulk Fermentation & Gluten Development (~4 to 5 Hours at 72°F–75°F)

1. Over the first 2 hours, perform 4 sets of coil folds (every 30 minutes) to build strong gluten structure without tearing the dough.
2. Let the dough rest undisturbed for the remaining 2 to 3 hours until it rises by about 50%–75%, looks puffy around the edges, and shows small bubbles underneath.

## Step 3: Preshape & Bench Rest (25 Minutes)

1. Turn the dough onto a lightly floured counter. Gently tuck the edges underneath to form a loose round.
2. Let it rest uncovered for 20–25 minutes so the gluten relaxes.

## Step 4: Final Shaping (Preventing Spread in a Wide Pot)

Because your large Lodge pot has a wide floor, the dough won't lean on the walls for vertical support—surface tension is everything:

1. Lightly flour the top of the resting dough, then flip it over so the tacky side faces up.
2. Gently stretch the dough into a rough square. Fold the bottom third up, then fold the left and right sides over like a letter.
3. Roll the dough up tightly from the bottom into a snug cylinder, then cup your hands around the back edge and pull it toward you across an unfloured section of the counter.
4. Repeat this "drag and tuck" motion 3–4 times, rotating a quarter turn each time, until the outer skin looks taut and smooth.
5. Place seam-side up in a well-floured proofing basket (or a bowl lined with a rice-floured tea towel).

## Step 5: Cold Proof (Overnight, 12–16 Hours)

1. Cover the basket and refrigerate overnight at 38°F–40°F. Cold dough holds its round shape dramatically better when transferred into a wide Dutch oven and scores much more cleanly.

## Step 6: Bake in Your Lodge Dutch Oven

1. **Preheat:** Put your empty Lodge pot (with lid on) into a cold oven and heat to 475°F for 45 minutes. Place a heavy sheet pan on the rack below the Dutch oven to shield the bottom crust from scorching.
2. **Score:** Flip the cold dough onto a parchment paper sling. Use a bread lame at a 30-degree angle to make one long, confident slash (about ½-inch deep) across the top.
3. **Covered Bake:** Lower the dough into the hot pot via the parchment sling, cover with the lid, drop the oven temp to 450°F, and bake for 20 minutes.
4. **Uncovered Bake:** Remove the lid and bake at 450°F for 20–25 minutes until deep mahogany brown and the internal temp reaches 208°F.
5. **Cool:** Transfer to a wire rack and cool completely for at least 1.5–2 hours before slicing so the crumb sets.
